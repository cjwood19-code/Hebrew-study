Difficulty progression

Pronunciation: when Hebrew is visible, tap the 🔊 button to hear it with the device's Hebrew voice. Audio is on demand and never affects scoring. Missing-word sentences keep audio hidden until the Hebrew sentence is completed so the sound does not reveal the answer.

Running accuracy is your cumulative percentage across all scored lesson and checkpoint questions, so it changes as you progress. Optional Rapid Recognition and Script Practice do not affect it.

Level accuracy tracks your cumulative scored performance for the current level. The Review number is the count of items still awaiting mastery. An item enters review when it is missed and stays there until you answer that item correctly 10 times; its progress is shown as x/10 below.

Sentence mastery applies only when the question itself is a Hebrew sentence with a missing word. Those questions have a required English-translation second part. A sentence is retired after 5 correct Hebrew completions and 5 correct English translations, then a new sentence is added. English-prompt sentence-building questions remain one-part exercises.

Connector questions also use two parts: first select the missing connector, then translate the completed Hebrew sentence into English.

Grammar questions that present a complete Hebrew sentence with a missing word also use the same two-part format. Short word/phrase grammar drills remain one-part.

Infinitive mastery is tracked separately in the optional Verb Infinitive Quiz. Only Hebrew infinitives are tested. A verb reaches mastery after 10 correct selections; after that it appears only once every 7 calendar days for reinforcement.

Conjugated verb matching is a separate weighted game. Every distinct past, present, and future form in the verb table is used. Missed forms reappear more often, and each form reaches mastery after 10 correct matches.

Each regular lesson has 15 scored questions. Above 85% advances one level. 70–85% repeats the level with missed words weighted more heavily. Below 70% starts a focused review at the same level. There is no final level. After Level 12, advanced levels continue indefinitely by cycling through sentence comprehension, matching, grammar/connectors, verbs, sentence building, handwriting, and cumulative challenge while drawing new/random exercises from the available material. After every five levels (5, 10, 15, 20, and so on), a mandatory 20-question block test reviews the five levels just completed. You must score at least 85% (17/20) to unlock the next block. Missed test questions are saved for later review. After every three scored levels, a non-graded rapid-recognition review is flagged for you.

Reading exercises from the books are kept in Reading Practice. Full passages and their comprehension questions are not mixed into grammar, verb, or ordinary lesson practice. Cumulative review can still revisit learned language, while the source reading activity stays together as a reading activity.

Book exercises are the primary practice source. The trainer now preserves actual exercises from your Level B books and class notes—including fill-in-the-blank reading/context items, tense conversion, singular/plural conversion, סמיכות, שם הפעולה, double-meaning vocabulary, and sentence transformations. Adaptive/generated questions remain supplemental reinforcement rather than replacing the source exercises.

The practice pool follows your two Level B Hebrew books and your teacher-created class notes, including the grammar and vocabulary actually covered in class.
